About JMFgames

JMFgames is a free pocket-console game system. When you open jmfgames.com, you'll see the JMF Games console — now in Game Boy Advance SP style, with a bigger 240×160 full-colour screen, richer graphics, and games rebuilt to be genuinely fun to play. Pick a game on the screen and play it with the D-pad and buttons. Every game is free to play in your web browser — no signup, no download, and no real money involved.

The console currently has fifteen games, listed in alphabetical order on the menu:

  • 8 Ball Pool — real 8-ball pool on a real table with real numbered balls and real physics: friction, spin/english, cushion bounces, and momentum transfer. Pick one of five AI opponents — Shark (Precision), Bruiser (Power), Ghost (Tactics), Trickster (Angles), or Ace (All-round) — each with a genuinely different playing style: Shark takes pots Ghost passes up for safeties, Ghost hides the cue ball, Trickster plays real bank shots with spin. Sink your group (solids or stripes, decided by the first ball pocketed), then the 8-ball, to win. Master seven trick-shot challenges, build a win streak, and rematch your rival. Left/Right aims, Up/Down sets power, A shoots, B fine-aims (and sets spin).
  • Blackjack Royale — classic blackjack against the dealer, now with two AI table-mates (ROSA and MAX) who play real basic strategy — sharp at the High Roller table, sloppy at the low table — so the table feels alive. Start with $1,000 and be the first to $10,000. Win-streak bonuses, a High Roller table that unlocks at $2,500, and persistent achievements. Dealer stands on all 17; 3:2 blackjack payout; split pairs.
  • BlockQuest — a block puzzle on a bigger 9×9 board. Fill rows and columns to clear them and score — now with combo chains that multiply your bonus, a Hold slot (B swaps pieces), levels every 6 lines cleared, and a +200 Perfect Clear bonus for emptying the whole board. The piece generator adapts to how you're playing: bigger pieces when you're on a roll, smaller ones when you're struggling. A fresh tray always contains a fitting piece when one exists.
  • Chess Club — chess against the computer, with five difficulty levels that genuinely play at their strength (Easy 800 to Grandmaster 2400) — real evaluation, tactics (forks, pins, skewers), and endgame play. Now with tactics puzzles (including a daily puzzle), a player Elo rating that moves with your results, win streaks, and a hint (B, −10 rating). Full rules: castling, en passant, check, checkmate, stalemate, promotion.
  • Emberfall Brawl — a roguelite auto-battler. Pick one of five heroes — BRAM, KIRA, ASH, plus new NOVA (Ember Mage) and ORIN (Stone Guardian) — and your hero fights on his own with smart AI. Enemies now focus your weakest fighter, and the Ember Lord boss every 5th wave has three learnable, telegraphed attack patterns. Hero-pair synergies boost your team when you summon. You make the calls: A summons the other heroes to help for 3 seconds (20-second cooldown), B unleashes your hero's ultimate (10-second cooldown), and between waves you choose upgrades. Survive as many waves as you can — one death ends the run. Run stats and per-hero records are tracked.
  • Harvest Haven — an original farming simulation, rebuilt for the bigger GBA screen. Inherit a run-down farm in the town of Maplewood — now five times bigger, with districts spread out and a camera that follows you — and rebuild it: till soil, plant seasonal crops, water them daily, and harvest to earn gold. Fish in the pond, mine the hills for ore, forage the forest, and befriend over two hundred townspeople — nine of whom you can marry — who now visibly walk around town with their own schedules and lives, each with a distinct look (hair, hat, role accessory, skin tone), not just a different shirt. Name your farmer, follow the main story in chapters (tracked in a Story Log), and play through each marriage candidate's story arc at 2/4/6/8/10 hearts. Every townsperson has a job — blacksmith, fisher, cook, teacher, doctor, merchant, and more — and they live full lives: they farm their own plots, fish, mine, take bulletin-board requests, befriend and marry each other, and raise children. Compete with the rival farmer Garth at the Fall Harvest Festival crop contest and climb the Town Rankings. Four seasonal festivals, animals to raise (chickens, cows, pigs, sheep, goats, and ducks), a pet dog or cat, artisan machines, a kitchen, tool upgrades, sprinklers, fertilizer and crop quality, fruit trees, beehives, a greenhouse, a beach with ocean fishing, a deeper mine with monsters to fight, weather, a museum, Town Hall restoration, a tropical island by boat, skill levels, and a story quest to restore Maplewood. D-pad walks, A uses the selected tool or talks, B cycles tools, Start pauses. Progress saves automatically, and you can delete your save to start over.
  • Overlord's Bastion — a lane siege battle. Command your troops against the Saint and destroy their castle. Four mirrored troop tiers on each side — Beast against Knight, Gorgon against Mage, Demon against Champion, Devil against Hero — plus two new units per side (Archer↔Ranger and Guardian↔Paladin) with a real counter web. The Saint stays aggressive (same 8/s mana as you, never toned down) but now plays smart: it counters your troops by lane, times its pushes, manages its mana, and adapts to your strategy. Take on the 5-Saint campaign — Apprentice to Grand Saint — or a Quick Battle. Both castles defend every lane, so battles last much longer.
  • RAGDOLL RUCKUS — Fling STITCH the burlap ragdoll around a toy-room arena with 9 unlockable weapons, from Fists to a Bomb — plus the new WHIP and ANVIL. STITCH now fights back smart: he dodges telegraphed swings, sidesteps projectiles, and counters when you overcommit. Real ragdoll physics: jointed limbs, momentum, bounce, and impact-scaled spin. Chain hits into combos to earn coins, complete four challenges, and unlock bigger toys. D-pad aims, A hits (or buys), B cycles weapons, Start pauses.
  • Sky Soar — fly through the pipes and see how far you can go. Now with real flight physics (gravity, flap momentum, terminal velocity, wind gusts), a PERFECT gap bonus, ramping pipe speed, and five unlockable birds — EMBER at 10, AZURE at 25, GOLDEN at 50, SHADOW at 100. A flaps, B dives.
  • Slingshot Siege — an original slingshot siege game with real projectile physics: gravity, a visible wind that changes per stage and bends your shot, bounce, and different projectile weights. Aim the slingshot, set your power, and launch stones, boulders, and bombs — plus new BOUNCE (ricochets) and SPLIT (bursts into three) projectiles — at enemy forts across one hundred challenge stages. Every 10th stage is a boss stage with a 3-HP armoured boss on its own keep. Destroy every target to clear the stage and unlock the next. Left/Right aims, Up/Down sets power, A launches, B cycles projectiles. Progress saves automatically, and you can delete your save to start over.
  • Solitaire — classic Klondike solitaire with real cards. Build the tableau down in alternating colors and stack the foundations up by suit from Ace to King. Now with scoring (foundation +10, uncovering cards +5, win bonus), win streaks, a daily deal (the same shuffle for everyone each day), a live timer, and a smart hint (B) that ranks every legal move and explains why — uncovering a face-down card beats a safe foundation move, and it warns you when a foundation move is risky. D-pad moves the cursor, A picks up and drops cards, B cancels (or hints).
  • STAR STRIKE — Blast waves of invaders in this vertical space shooter: drones, fighters, tanks and kamikazes, with a boss flagship every 5 waves. Enemies now fly in formations, dodge your shots, and bosses have three phases with new attack patterns each. Between waves, spend your score in the Upgrade Shop (Engine, Rapid Fire, Armor, Bombs, Lives). Your ship has real inertia — thrust accelerates, drag slows it, no instant stops. Grab power-ups for triple shot, shields, bombs and extra lives. D-pad flies, A fires (hold to auto-fire), B drops a bomb, Start pauses.
  • Subway Runner — an endless runner down the subway tracks with real jump and slide physics: a true jump arc, smooth lane glides (no teleporting), and slide momentum. Dodge trains, jump barriers and gaps, slide under overhead bars, and grab coins across twelve challenge stages — each with its own goal to clear to unlock the next — plus a daily stage. New power-ups (MAGNET, SHIELD, 2X COINS) and moving barriers. Every row is guaranteed passable, and the difficulty adapts to how you're playing. Left/Right switches lanes, Up jumps, Down slides. Progress saves automatically, and you can delete your save to start over.
  • Texas Hold'em — heads-up poker against the computer, now with a real poker AI: it evaluates hand strength and draws, calls and folds on true pot odds, sizes bets by strength and position, bluffs about 12% of the time, and adapts to how you play (bluffs more against folders, value-bets bigger against callers) — and it can never see your cards. The AI's hole cards stay face-down until showdown. Start with $1,000 and be the first to $10,000 — or win a tournament as the blinds climb every 5 hands. Win-streak bonuses and achievements.
  • Thrill Park — a theme park manager on a wider park. Build your own park: 18 rides, food and gift stalls, restrooms, First Aid, trash cans, and decorations, plus cleaners, engineers, entertainers, and security to hire. Guests are now real people with preferences — thrill-seekers, families, and kids choose rides by their mood and the queue, get hungry, thirsty, tired, and bored, and leave (or spread the word) based on how good your park is. Pick a scenario (Classic, Tiny Budget, Thrill Capital, Family Park), unlock the biggest rides by raising your park rating, and host VIP visits. Guests walk real paths and queue properly. Work through ten goals to become a Park Legend. D-pad moves the cursor, A places, B cycles what to build, Start pauses. Progress saves automatically, and you can delete your save to start over.

On a keyboard, the arrow keys (or WASD) work as the D-pad, X is the A button, Z is the B button, Enter is Start, and Shift is Menu. The Menu button takes you back to the main menu from anywhere, and the B button does something different in each game. Every game has background music, and the SOUND and MUSIC buttons below the Menu and Start buttons on the console turn the sound effects and the music on or off.
